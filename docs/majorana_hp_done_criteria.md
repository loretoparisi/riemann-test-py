# Majorana's infinite-component tower as a Hilbert-Polya frame: done criteria (written before building)

## The idea being tested

Majorana's infinite-component equations (1932, and the (2+1)-d version of Plyushchay) live in unitary
lowest-weight representations D+_k of SL(2, R) (here k is the Bargmann index: K0 = k + n on level n). Each level is
a mass state; the compact generator K0 gives Majorana's discrete mass spectrum m k/(k + n). The two *noncompact*
generators (boosts) have continuous spectrum on the whole real line.

The combined norm of the earlier runs (Lean: GRH(chi) <=> finite for all alpha in (3/4, 1)) has a Mellin side
(1/2pi) int |Gamma(c + iE)/L(1 - 2c - 2iE, chi)|^2 dE on the line Re z = c = 1 - alpha. The weight |Gamma(c + iE)|^2 dE
is exactly the spectral measure of the boost K1 = (K+ + K-)/2 of D+_k on its lowest state when k = c (a classical
fact: the Jacobi matrix of K1 in the level basis is the Meixner-Pollaczek recurrence). At the critical value
k = 1/4 (alpha = 3/4) the representation is the even sector of one harmonic oscillator, and the boost is
-(xp + px)/4, i.e. the Berry-Keating operator xp up to the factor -1/2. The arithmetic vector is a Moebius
superposition of dilated (squeezed) oscillator ground states: M_chi(x^2) = sum mu chi(n)/n e^{-x^2/n^2}.

So: the combined norm is <v0| |L(1 - 2k - 2i K1, chi)|^{-2} |v0> in Majorana's representation D+_k. As k -> 1/4 the
factor 1/|L|^2 develops peaks of width ~ 1/4 - k at E = -gamma/2 for every zero 1/2 + i gamma, so after
renormalising by h = 1/4 - k the spectral measure should become a pure point measure supported on the zeros
on the critical line, with total mass the blow-up constant B_chi = (1/8) sum_rho |Gamma((1-rho)/2)/L'(rho)|^2. Its
truncations (finite Majorana towers) are then finite self-adjoint matrices whose eigenvalues converge to the
zeros: a Hilbert-Polya-type construction.

What it cannot do, stated before testing: the operator is built from L itself, and a zero off the line at
beta + i gamma appears only in the space D+_k with k = (1 - beta)/2, not at k = 1/4. Showing that no D+_k with
k < 1/4 carries point spectrum is GRH again. Check 5 tests this limit with a model L. All of it is numerical
evidence about known zeros of low height, not a proof.

A check is CONSISTENT only if every item in its row holds, with the key numbers from two independent
computations (two truncations, two discretisations or a closed form).
